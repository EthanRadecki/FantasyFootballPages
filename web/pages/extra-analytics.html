Championship Gauntlet
Who had to earn it?
How It Works

Each title run is scored on three things: points opponents actually scored against the champion, how strong those opponents were across the regular season, and whether they were hot or cold in the five games right before facing the champion. Every score is measured against the full history of the league, so the scale stays fixed as new champions get added. Three-game and four-game runs get a small statistical adjustment to compare fairly. Weighted 70/15/15, heavily toward how tough the games themselves actually were.

Gauntlet Score blends three z-scored components: points scored against the champion, opponent regular-season dominance, and opponent hot streak entering the game. Each is measured against the full league history and mapped through a fixed curve, not scaled to just these runs. Games are lightly shrunk toward the league average based on how many rounds each champion played, so three-game and four-game runs compare fairly. Weighted 70/15/15, so this is mostly about how tough the actual games were, with dominance and hot streak adding a smaller layer of nuance. A score near 50 is a typical path. Anything further out means the field was tougher or softer than usual.

Schedule Luck
Who has the schedule been kind to?
How It Works

Each week, every manager's score is compared to the league median. If you scored above the median, you "should have" won that week regardless of your actual opponent. The difference between your actual wins and these expected wins is your schedule luck. Positive means the schedule gifted you wins; negative means it took them away.

Win% Attribution
Where does a season's win% actually come from?
Methodology

Five ingredients, each pulled from the metric that already powers its own page: draft surplus (Surplus Value), waiver upside (only the positive value from pickups counts, bad and neutral adds contribute nothing), missed wins from Lineup Efficiency (a bad start/sit call only counts if it actually flipped a real result), trade QUAD (Trade Value), and schedule luck (regular season only, real bracket games excluded same as consolation weeks). The outcome being explained, Win%, includes real playoff bracket games (still no consolation games), which schedule luck itself deliberately doesn't: a shrinking bracket field breaks the weekly-median comparison luck depends on in a way it doesn't break a plain win/loss count. A season-level regression () turns these into a per-manager breakdown of exactly how many points of win%, above or below league average, each ingredient is responsible for.
