Methodology
  • Optimal lineup: the best possible score from a manager's full roster that week, following real slot rules ().
  • IR players are excluded. Bye weeks and consolation weeks are excluded too.
  • Excluded from the average: weeks with 2+ mandatory slots left empty despite a real bench alternative. Still counts as a missed win if applicable. A single empty slot stays in as normal data.
  • Bench depth: weekly bench value compared to other benches at the same position, summed across the bench, averaged over time. Measures roster talent, not decision quality. Example: a score of 3.99 means a manager's whole bench, added together, nets out to about 4 points above what a league-average bench would post that week. It is not "each bench player is 4 points better."

Depth-Adjusted Efficiency
A low raw gap can mean sharp decisions, or it can mean a thin bench that never gave you a chance to miss. This adjusts for that.
How To Read This Chart

Bench depth correlates with raw efficiency gap by about %. That means depth alone predicts a large share of a manager's average gap, before looking at a single lineup decision. So we use that relationship to predict what a manager's gap should be, given their depth, then take the difference between that predicted gap and the manager's actual gap. That difference is depth-adjusted efficiency. A lower value means the model says you made better lineup decisions than your bench depth alone would suggest.
