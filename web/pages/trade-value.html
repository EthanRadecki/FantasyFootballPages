Methodology
  • Trade Grade (z-score): value swing: what you got minus what you gave, position-adjusted.
  • Realized Gains (z-score): same swing, counting only weeks the player was started.
  • Fit Score (z-score shift): team's positional output, before the trade vs. after.
  • Necessity Score (points/week, shrunk for small samples): acquired player vs. best unstarted bench option that week.
  • Playoff Weighting (1.0x–2.0x multiplier): production counts more the later it happens: Regular 1.0x, First Round 1.15x, Quarterfinal 1.3x, Semifinal 1.6x, Championship 2.0x.
  • QUAD (z-score): the four metrics combined, weighted 35/30/20/15 (Realized Gains/Trade Grade/Fit/Necessity).

Note: reversed trades are excluded entirely, multi-team trades are linked and graded on each manager's true net position.

Manager Leaderboard
Average or total QUAD per manager, career or by season.

Trade count is shown in each manager's label. Hover a row for that manager's best and worst trade in this view. Average measures decision quality per trade regardless of volume. Total measures aggregate value gained or lost through trading overall, so a manager who trades often weighs in more here even at a modest average.

Trade Quality vs. Win Percentage
Does making good trades actually correlate with winning more than making a lot of them?

Each bubble is one manager, positioned by average QUAD per trade (x-axis, trade quality) and career win percentage (y-axis, bracket-only games, consolation weeks excluded). Bubble size is trades made, so volume is still visible even though it's not an axis. The dashed line is the best fit across all managers. Trade quality correlates with winning more strongly than trade volume does, though it's still far from the whole story, a lot of winning comes down to the draft, waiver wire, and week-to-week lineup decisions covered elsewhere on this site.
