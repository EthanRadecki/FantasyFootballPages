How to Read This

Every manager-season is scored on 20 drafting signals, distilled here into 10 non-redundant traits. The radar below normalizes each trait against the field for that season (outer edge = that year's league max), so the shapes compare drafting style, not draft-year inflation. Toggle managers on and off, switch between seasons or career, and hover any point for the raw number behind it. All data covers 2020-2025, 85 manager-seasons across 16 managers.

Draft Archetypes
K-means (k=4, on 10 PCA components) groups manager-seasons into four drafting styles.
Important Notes

This reflects draft-day decisions only, not waivers, trades, injuries, or lineup management. It's also a soft grouping: silhouette scores were low across every k tested (0.09–0.17), meaning this is a continuous behavioral space, not sharply separated tribes. A bootstrap resample (200 iterations) put average agreement with the original clustering at 0.53 (ARI), ranging from 0.26 to 0.84 across resamples: real signal, but plenty of manager-seasons sit near a boundary rather than deep inside one archetype. And because 14 of the league's 16 managers show up in more than one archetype across their seasons, archetype behaves like a season-level style choice, not a fixed personal trait.

Cluster Scatter
Each dot is one manager-season, colored by archetype. Did better drafting actually lead to more wins?

X axis: surplus per pick that season. Y axis: win%. The four quadrants are purely descriptive: Kruskal-Wallis tests found no significant link between archetype and either Win% or PPG (see Archetype Outcomes below), so no quadrant should be read as "the draft caused the record."

Archetype Outcomes
How did each drafting style translate to on-field results?

Kruskal-Wallis tests found no significant difference in Win% (p=0.232) or PPG (p=0.568) across archetypes. Bars and line show the averages anyway for reference, though the differences you see here are not distinguishable from noise. Methodology
How this was built, and where to be skeptical of it.

Feature set & reduction

20 z-scored features per manager-season fed a PCA reduced to 10 components (85% variance retained) before clustering. PCA was chosen specifically to neutralize a real multicollinearity cluster: Reach Tendency, Value Hunting, Draft Conviction, and ADP Independence pairwise-correlate at r=0.73–0.84, likely one underlying ADP-deviation signal wearing four names.

Choosing k=4

Elbow + silhouette sweep (k=2–9) cross-validated against Ward-linkage hierarchical clustering. k=4 was the point where K-means and hierarchical clustering agreed most (ARI=0.50), the elbow curve started flattening, and k=5 stopped avoiding a degenerate 3-observation cluster. It's a defensible choice, not an obvious one: k=3 is a reasonable alternative, and an independent GMM run on the same PCA components slightly preferred k=3 (silhouette 0.162 vs 0.146 for k=4).

Imputation

K and DST ADP-deviation were missing for 7 and 2 of 85 rows respectively, since those picks (rounds 13–16) fell outside the tracked ADP universe. Both were imputed via linear regression on the corresponding patience score (k_patience → k_adp_deviation, r=0.79; dst_patience → dst_adp_deviation, r=0.62), with every imputed value falling inside the column's observed range.

Known exclusions & cautions

  • Thomas Sullivan and William Serafin (both 2020-only) are excluded from surplus-based analysis; no surplus join is available for that season.
  • Avg ADP Deviation's sign convention (positive = reached vs. waited) wasn't re-derived from source formulas this session. Treat it as a relative ranking, not an absolute direction.
  • Managers are not independent across seasons: 14 of 16 appear in more than one archetype. Cross-archetype performance comparisons should be read with that in mind.
