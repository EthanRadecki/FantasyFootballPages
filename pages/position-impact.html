How Much Does Each Position Matter?

The same "remove it and recheck the winner" method from the defense analysis, run for every position -- QB, RB, WR, TE, K, and D/ST, side by side.

Methodology

For every real matchup since 2020 (regular season plus real playoff bracket games, no consolation weeks -- 598 games total), each position's started points get subtracted from both teams' scores to see how often the winner would change, how predictable that position is week-to-week, and how well a team's production at that position predicts their actual win%. For the draft-capital analysis, QB/TE/K/D-ST use the round each manager's first pick at that position was taken; RB/WR use the second pick, since nearly everyone takes their RB1/WR1 in the first couple rounds regardless of philosophy -- the second one is where actual draft strategy diverges.

Which Position Decides The Most Games?

% of all 598 games that would have had a different winner if that position didn't exist.

Which Position Is Hardest To Predict?

Coefficient of variation: how much a player's own week-to-week score swings relative to their own average. Taller bars mean less predictable.

Which Position's Performance Predicts Winning Most?

Correlation (r) between a team's season-long production at that position and their regular-season win%, across 83 manager-seasons.

Does Draft Round Predict Winning, For Any Position?

Correlation (r) between the round a manager's Nth pick at that position was drafted and their season win%. Every position lands close to zero -- position performance matters (see above), but when you draft it barely does, across the board.

Every point ever scored at each position, league-wide, by how it was acquired.
